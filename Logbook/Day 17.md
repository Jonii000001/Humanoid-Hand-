Day 17: Magnet issues and fixing

Today I made a really big step forward with the joint of the finger. I figured out how I can read precise the angel axis.

At the end of the day, I decided to configure the size of the ball bearing, because I had trouble with the diametrical magnet. After all, the bearing would affect the angle of the encoder. The reason for the wrong axis is because of ferromagnetic fields, and I have to figure out how I can solve that.

First, I tried to place the magnet outside of the bearing with a 0,5mm distance wall so that the ferromagnetic rays couldn't fool the encoder, but if I did that like in the picture, the joint of the finger would be 31mm broad, and I think that's too much; a normal person has a joint like 20 mm, maximum 22mm.

So, I went trough my thoughts in my head and came to the idea, what if I change the size from the ball bearing to the outside diameter of 11mm and the inside diameter to 7mm then I could fit the magnet with a diameter of 6mm inside it and design a wall at the outside, out of plastic (PETG-Filament) and the ferromagnetic fields wouldn't disturb the magnet.

I hope I don't have a wrong idea, and hopefully it'll be a brilliant one and work. Because if that works, I could fit the AS5048A encoder directly at the outside of the finger joint on the plastic, and it would take like only 3- 4 mm outdoor distance, and then the finger would be ca. 25mm broad. I hope the finger won't look so unnaturally big like a block.

If it works, I can perfectly read the angle, and this means the finger will be perfectly calibrated, and no axis errors will have an influence on the magnet encoder chip. By the way, I had to look for a diametral magnet for almost 1 hour, because I only found 1 customer who sells such a magnet. I almost gave up looking for one. But I want to reach my goal and build a perfect humanoid hand.
